# Ad hook procedure

A hook is the first three seconds of a short-form video ad. Write five, each from a
different angle, so the merchant can test rather than guess.

Angles, one hook each:

1. **Problem first** — name the annoyance the product removes, in the buyer's words.
2. **Demo** — show the product doing the one thing it does best; the hook is the result.
3. **Social proof** — a real-sounding customer line or a concrete number the merchant gave you. Never invent reviews, awards, or statistics.
4. **Founder** — why the merchant made this; one honest sentence to camera.
5. **Offer** — the launch deal, stated plainly, with the deadline.

Rules:

- Twelve words or fewer per hook. Spoken, not written: read it aloud in your head.
- No claims the merchant did not give you (no "clinically proven", no "#1").
- Pair every hook with a one-line shot description a phone can film in a kitchen.
- Match the hook to the funnel: problem-first and demo hooks for cold audiences in
  the test week, offer hooks for retargeting.
- End with which two hooks to film first and why.
